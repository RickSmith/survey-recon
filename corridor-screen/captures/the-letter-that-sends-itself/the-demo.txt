  The letter that sends itself
  ============================

  Right of entry is not a statutory right in Texas. You have to ask,
  and when nobody answers you have to ask again. TxDOT ships a second
  request letter template because non-response is the ordinary case.

  The tract, read out of project-sh16/screening.json
    parcel        15664-003-0040
    owner         EPISCOPAL CHURCH CORP IN WEST TEXAS
    on it         cemetery (on)
    longest wait  14 calendar days (cemetery)

  Where day 21 comes from
    No published right-of-entry turn-around was found in any TxDOT
    manual this repo read, so there is no number to quote. The
    interval is derived from the checked-in lead-time table instead:

      longest confirmed wait, counted in calendar days   45 days
        set by the railroad row, cited to https://www.up.com/real-estate/tempuse/procedures
      half of it, so letter two has the window letter one had   22 days
      rounded down to whole weeks, because a letter moves in weeks   21 days (3 weeks)

    Change the table and this day changes. There is no 21 written anywhere in the tool.

    The railroad row sets it, and no railroad was found on this corridor.
    That is deliberate. The interval is how long a letter may sit
    before you ask again, not what is on this one tract -- so it
    comes from the longest wait the firm plans against anywhere.

  The clock, with nobody watching it
    2026-09-19   day 0   request 1 of 2 written
    2026-10-09   day 20  nothing due
    2026-10-10   day 21  request 2 of 2 written -- no human action

  21 days is 3 whole weeks, so letter two falls on the same weekday as letter one. Both
  land on a Saturday. The clock counts calendar days and knows nothing about weekends or
  holidays. It writes the letter; a person still sends it.

  Nobody has to run this
    .github/workflows/roe-followup.yml reads the same clock every day
    on a schedule. It commits nothing, mails nothing and needs no
    secret. If request 2 of 2 turns up in its summary on 2026-10-10
    and nobody started it, that is the claim above, evidenced.

  What this does not do
    It does not mail anything. It has no address and no authority.
    An RPLS signs the letter and remains accountable for it --
    22 Tex. Admin. Code 131.2(38).
