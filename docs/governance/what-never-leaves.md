# Before you type it into an AI tool

## The one question

**Would I email this outside the firm?**

If no, it does not go in. That is the whole rule. Everything below is that rule,
with the arguments already had.

**An AI tool sends what you type to a company's computers.** That is how it
answers. It is not a program running on your desk.

---

## NEVER — no exception, no "just this once"

- [ ] **Client names.** Also addresses, phone numbers, email addresses
- [ ] **Anything that identifies a client** even without the name — an unusual
      tract, a pending sale, a dispute anyone local would recognise
- [ ] **Unrecorded documents.** Deeds not yet filed, title commitments, easements
      in negotiation
- [ ] **Anything under a confidentiality or non-disclosure agreement**
- [ ] **Anything under a protective order or a litigation hold**
- [ ] **Field data from a live job.** Point files, raw observations, field notes,
      photographs
- [ ] **Drawings from a live job.** Including a screenshot of one
- [ ] **Our pricing.** Rates, build-ups, anything you would not show a competitor
- [ ] **Anything about an employee.** Pay, performance, health, complaints
- [ ] **Passwords, licence keys, or anything that gets somebody into a system**
- [ ] **«ADD YOURS»**

## ALLOWED — go ahead

- [ ] **Recorded public documents.** Filed plats, deeds of record
- [ ] **County appraisal district data.** It is published
- [ ] **Published standards, manuals and statutes.** TxDOT, TBPELS, the codes
- [ ] **Anything already on our public website**
- [ ] **General questions with no job attached.** "How does TxDOT define primary
      control?" is fine. "How does TxDOT define primary control for the Henderson
      tract?" is not
- [ ] **A job with the identifying details replaced by placeholders** — see below
- [ ] **Anything a client has agreed in writing that we may use this way**
- [ ] **«ADD YOURS»**

## The placeholder trick

Most jobs can be worked with the identifying parts taken out.

| Instead of | Type |
|---|---|
| The client's name | `THE CLIENT` |
| The property address | `THE SUBJECT TRACT` |
| A specific parcel ID | `PARCEL A`, `PARCEL B` |
| Actual coordinates on a live job | A nearby public control point, or nothing |

**«RPLS NAME» puts the real details back in afterwards.**

If the tool cannot do useful work without the real thing, **that is your answer.
It is not a job for the tool.**

---

## Two traps that catch careful people

**Screenshots count.** A picture of a plat carries every name on it. So does a
photograph of a monument with a house number in the frame.

**Pasted-in files count.** Attaching or pasting a document sends all of it, not
the part you were looking at. Check what is further down the page before you
paste.

## Why this list looks the way it does

### The rule it rests on

**22 Tex. Admin. Code § 138.61** is titled "Surveyors Shall Maintain
Confidentiality of Clients." Subsection (a):

> The surveyor may reveal confidences and private information only with a fully
> informed client's or employer's consent, or when required by law or court order;
> or when those confidences, if left undisclosed, would constitute a threat to the
> health, safety, or welfare of the public.

Subsection (c) is the one that makes this a wall poster rather than a personal
resolution:

> The surveyor shall exercise reasonable care to prevent unauthorized disclosure or
> use of private information or confidences concerning a client or employer by the
> surveyor's employees and associates.

**You are on the hook for what your staff discloses.** A list on the wall is what
"reasonable care" looks like from the outside.

### The board has said this specifically about AI

From [PAO 71](seal-and-responsible-charge.md), approved 14 November 2024:

> Licensees should also be aware of how any AI software utilizes and stores data
> that is provided to it to ensure that the confidentiality of any client's data is
> maintained.

Note the verb: **be aware of how the software stores data.** That is a question to
ask the vendor in writing, and to ask again when you change tools.

### The $1,000 figure

TBPELS's table of suggested sanctions for surveyors sets the starting sanction for
"Reveal confidences and private information," under § 138.61(a), (b) and (c), at
**reprimand and $1,000**. The board may impose more or less.

**Source:** 22 Tex. Admin. Code § 139.37(b), as printed in the TBPELS *Acts and
Rules* effective 18 June 2026, page 110 —
<https://pels.texas.gov/downloads/lawrules.pdf>.

That is the board's part alone. It says nothing about the client, the contract, or
your carrier.

### What is a firm choice here, and what is not

**The rule** is § 138.61, and it is broad: client confidences and private
information.

**The specific list** — pricing, employee information, screenshots, the
placeholder method — is **our choice**, not a legal requirement. It is drawn from
what the rule protects and from what actually goes wrong. Adapt it. Just do not
let anyone tell you the list is the law; the law is the rule it came from.

## What we could not confirm

- **Whether any board rule names specific data types that may not go into
  software.** **Not found.** We read the TBPELS *Acts and Rules* effective
  18 June 2026 and the full policy advisory opinion index at
  <https://pels.texas.gov/policy.htm> on 2026-09-12. The obligation is stated as a
  standard — confidentiality and reasonable care — and not as a list. The list is
  ours
- **What any particular AI vendor does with what you type.** **Not checked, and
  not checkable from here.** It differs by vendor, by plan, and by settings, and
  it changes. Ask your vendor in writing and keep the answer with this policy
