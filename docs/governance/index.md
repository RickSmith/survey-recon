# Governance

Three documents to take home. One for your insurer, one for your wall, and one
for the argument about who is responsible.

They are **templates and a reading of public sources, not legal advice**, and not
TBPELS guidance. Have your own counsel and your carrier read them. What they do
is save those people an hour and give them something to mark up.

## Start here: the board has already spoken

**TBPELS approved a written opinion on AI on 14 November 2024.** It is
[Policy Advisory Opinion 71](https://pels.texas.gov/nm/2024/pao-71-response.pdf),
two pages, and if you read nothing else on this site, read that.

Its position, in short:

- **AI software is a tool.** Nothing in the Acts or the board rules bans it
- **Oversight and review by the licensee are not optional.** "Licensees are
  ultimately responsible for any work product they sign and seal"
- **Do not use it outside your own competence.** It is not a way into an
  unfamiliar kind of job
- **Know what the software does with your client's data**

The board concluded that no new opinion was needed, because the rules it already
has cover it. The [seal page](seal-and-responsible-charge.md) quotes the whole
thing and names the rules.

## The one sentence, if you are in a hurry

**You seal it, you own it.**

That was true before AI and it is true now. Two rules put it beyond argument, and
[the seal page](seal-and-responsible-charge.md) quotes both in full:
22 Tex. Admin. Code § 138.33(b), which lets you seal only work you did or
**directly supervised**, and § 131.2(11), which defines that supervision as
approving decisions **before they are acted on**.

Which is why the way of working this session teaches — a written scope, small
pieces, a check before anything is accepted — is not a software habit borrowed for
show. **It is the shape the rule already asks for.**

## What to do with these, in order

The three cards above are in browsing order, heaviest last. This is the order to
actually work through them.

1. **Read [the seal page](seal-and-responsible-charge.md) yourself.** Twenty
   minutes. It is the one with the citations, and you are the one who signs
2. **Print [the checklist](what-never-leaves.md) and pin it up.** Before anybody
   at your firm types anything into an AI tool. This is the cheapest thing on this
   site and the one with the fastest payback
3. **Adapt [the policy](ai-use-policy.md)**, then send it to your counsel and your
   carrier. Ask your carrier one question in writing: *does our cover change if AI
   tools are used on a job?*
4. **Diary a review.** PAO 71 says the board "will continue to monitor artificial
   intelligence software." Check <https://pels.texas.gov/policy.htm> every six
   months

## What these pages will not do

They will not tell you AI is safe, and they will not tell you to avoid it.

They will not stand in for your lawyer or your carrier.

They will not move the responsibility. **Nothing does.** That is the whole point
of the seal, and it is the reason a surveyor can be trusted with a boundary in the
first place.
