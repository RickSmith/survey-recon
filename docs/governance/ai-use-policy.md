# A firm AI-use policy you can adapt

This is a template. Copy it, fill in the blanks, cross out what is not true at
your firm, and put your name on it.

**It is not legal advice**, and it is not TBPELS guidance. It is a starting draft
so that the conversation with your counsel and your carrier begins from a
document rather than a blank page.

Every rule below is marked with what it rests on:

- **Rule** — a board rule or a statute requires it. The citation is given
- **Board guidance** — [PAO 71](seal-and-responsible-charge.md) tells you to do it
- **Firm choice** — nothing requires this. It is here because it works. Delete it
  if you disagree

That distinction is the point of the template. **Do not let anyone tell you a
firm choice is a requirement.**

!!! tip "Filling it in"
    Anything in «guillemets» is a blank. Replace it with your firm's answer and
    delete the brackets. If you get to the end and a blank is still empty, that
    is the part you have not decided yet — and it is usually the part that
    matters.

# «FIRM NAME» — Policy on the use of AI tools

**Effective «DATE». Approved by «RPLS NAME», «REGISTRATION NUMBER».**
**Next review «DATE + 6 MONTHS».**

## 1. Who is responsible

**«RPLS NAME» signs and seals everything that leaves this office.** That does not
change because an AI tool did some of the work.

An AI tool used here is a **tool**, in the same category as a data collector or a
drafting package. It is not a colleague, not a second opinion, not a co-signer,
and it cannot carry any part of the liability.

> *Rule.* 22 Tex. Admin. Code § 138.33(b): a surveyor may seal only work done by
> them or performed under their direct supervision. "Upon sealing, surveyors take
> full professional responsibility for that work."
> *Board guidance.* PAO 71: "Licensees are ultimately responsible for any work
> product they sign and seal."

## 2. What we use AI tools for

Approved here, today:

- «Desktop research on public records and published standards»
- «Drafting internal documents, checklists and correspondence»
- «Summarising long documents we already hold»
- «ADD YOUR OWN»

## 3. What we do not use AI tools for

- **Work in a field this firm is not already competent in.** An AI tool is not a
  way into an unfamiliar kind of job
- **Anything presented as final without a licensed review.** See section 5
- **Producing anything that looks sealed** — no seal images, no completed
  signature blocks, no "I hereby certify" language in a draft
- **«ADD YOUR OWN»**

> *Rule.* 22 Tex. Admin. Code § 138.59(a), (b): practise only in your areas of
> competence.
> *Board guidance.* PAO 71: "Licensees should not use AI software to work on a
> project in which they do not already have established competence through
> education or experience."

## 4. What never goes into an AI tool

An AI tool sends what you type to a company's computers so it can answer. **Treat
anything you give it as though you had emailed it outside this firm**, because
functionally you did.

The full list is on the wall. See
[What never leaves the office](what-never-leaves.md). The short version:

**Never:** anything identifying a client · unrecorded documents · anything under
a confidentiality agreement, protective order or litigation hold · live job field
data · our pricing.

**Allowed:** public records · published standards and statutes · anything already
on our website · anything a client has agreed in writing that we may use this way.

**When a job needs private information to proceed**, the tool works with a
placeholder and «RPLS NAME» fills it in afterwards. If the tool cannot do useful
work without the real thing, **this is not a job for the tool.**

> *Rule.* 22 Tex. Admin. Code § 138.61(a), (c): reveal client confidences only
> with informed consent or where the law requires, and exercise reasonable care to
> prevent unauthorised disclosure by employees and associates.
> *Board guidance.* PAO 71: "Licensees should also be aware of how any AI software
> utilizes and stores data that is provided to it to ensure that the
> confidentiality of any client's data is maintained."

## 5. How work comes back, and how it gets checked

**Nothing an AI tool produces leaves this office until «RPLS NAME» has read it
against its sources.**

The check is not a proofread. It is the same check you would give a new
employee's first submittal:

1. **Does every number have a source?** Follow at least «two» of them back to the
   original document, and confirm that document is current
2. **Are the "could not confirm" items still marked as such?** A draft that lost
   its question marks between versions has been quietly upgraded, and that is how
   an error gets sealed
3. **Is anything cited from a superseded source?** Retired manual addresses and
   old rule chapter numbers are still all over the internet. See the
   [citation trap](seal-and-responsible-charge.md#a-citation-trap-and-it-will-catch-you)
4. **Would you have reached the same conclusion?** If you cannot say yes, it does
   not go out

**If agent-assisted work must leave the office before the check is finished**, it
goes out as a preliminary document under 22 Tex. Admin. Code § 138.33(e), with
the exact caveat text that rule requires, unsigned and unsealed.

> *Rule.* 22 Tex. Admin. Code § 131.2(11): direct supervision means the surveyor
> "personally reviews and approves proposed decisions prior to their
> implementation."
> *Firm choice.* The four-point check and the number in step 1 are ours. Set your
> own number and write it down.

## 6. Work in small pieces

Work given to an AI tool is written down first, given in one piece at a time, and
checked at the end of each piece.

The reason is section 5. Approval before implementation is not possible on a job
that ran to the end unsupervised.

> *Firm choice*, resting on a rule. Nothing prescribes a job size. But § 131.2(11)
> asks you to approve decisions before they are acted on, and a large unsupervised
> job makes that impossible.

## 7. What we write down

For each job where an AI tool did substantive work, keep with the job file:

- **What was asked for** — the written scope given to the tool
- **What came back** — the draft, kept as received
- **What was checked, by whom, and on what date**
- **What was changed and why**

Keep it for «YOUR RECORD RETENTION PERIOD», the same as any other job record.

> *Firm choice.* We found no board rule requiring a record of AI use. This is here
> because the record is what shows your supervision was real, and the day you need
> it is the day someone disputes the work.

## 8. Telling people

- **Clients:** «our practice is / is not to disclose AI assistance on a job».
  Decide this once and apply it to everybody
- **Our carrier:** «RPLS NAME» will confirm with «CARRIER» that this policy is
  consistent with our cover, before «DATE»
- **Staff:** every person who touches an AI tool here reads this policy and signs
  below

> *Firm choice.* We found no rule requiring client disclosure of AI use, and no
> rule forbidding it. Whichever you choose, write it down and be consistent — an
> inconsistent practice is harder to defend than either consistent one.

## 9. When something goes wrong

If an AI tool produces something wrong and it reaches a client, or client
information goes somewhere it should not:

1. Tell «RPLS NAME» the same day. Not the next morning
2. Write down what happened, while you remember it
3. «RPLS NAME» decides what is told to the client and to «CARRIER»

**Nobody is disciplined here for reporting an AI error promptly.** People are
disciplined for hiding one.

## 10. Review

This policy is reviewed every «SIX MONTHS» by «RPLS NAME», and immediately if
TBPELS issues new guidance.

The board said in PAO 71 that it "will continue to monitor artificial intelligence
software and its impact on the Board's licensees and update and amend this policy
statement as warranted." **Check <https://pels.texas.gov/policy.htm> at each
review.**

## Before you adopt it

Three honest warnings.

**A policy with a rule nobody follows is worse than no policy.** Read it through
once more and cross out anything that is not true at your firm. You will be held
to what this says.

**Do not paste your own client information into an AI tool while adapting this
document.** The irony would not save you.

**Have your counsel and your carrier read it.** This template was drafted for a
conference session by people who are not your lawyers. What it does is save them
an hour and give them something to mark up.
